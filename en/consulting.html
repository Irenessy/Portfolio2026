TACONIC BIOSCIENCES · WECHAT EDITORIAL STRATEGY

Ongoing operations / channel proposal

What is worth publishing again next month?

A research article might help a reader discover a subject, explore it in depth or pass a useful finding to a colleague. I assign each piece a purpose in the calendar, then review the behaviour that would support it.

Planning the reading experience

Choose a question and a format

In the monthly calendar I record the intended reader, their question, the format and the review status. A long read leaves room for evidence. An illustrated digest helps a reader decide which subject deserves a closer look.

An October article on ALS

The bilingual draft covered a SOD1 rat model of ALS. It illustrates an editorial choice: identify the subject in the title, put the research question in the lead, then organise the evidence under behaviour, electrophysiology and biomarkers.

Before publication

I check abbreviations, the study population and figure captions against the source. Separating observations from possible applications keeps a summary of model research from suggesting an established effect in humans.

Inside the monthly review

I start with the month-on-month picture, then match changes to publication dates, sources and individual posts. Select a question to see how a signal can inform the next brief.

Read the signal

Split new follows by search, QR code and article, then align daily changes with the publication calendar.

Check another explanation

If new follows came mostly through QR codes, check campaign or event activity on those dates before crediting a post published that day.

Design the next comparison

If search keeps bringing in readers, retain explicit topic terms in comparable briefs and check the source mix over the same time window.

Read the signal

Read click-through and completion together. The first helps assess the entry point. The second shows whether the body held attention.

Check another explanation

Compare posts of similar length and format. A research feature and a visual digest invite different reading behaviour. Check the denominator and observation window.

Design the next comparison

If many open but few finish, keep the subject and revise the lead and subheads. Review both measures against the next comparable piece.

Read the signal

Read shares alongside readership, then inspect the summary or graphic a reader might actually forward to a colleague.

Check another explanation

A small audience can produce an unusually high share rate. Report counts with rates and distinguish an individual post from the cumulative trend.

Design the next comparison

Try a summary card for a reference-heavy topic. Record the change and compare sharing on similar posts.

Taking the editorial work to a new channel

The recommendation

I reviewed public campaigns from Cyagen and Beyotime and proposed a small organic Rednote pilot using approved WeChat image digests. Reusing reviewed material makes audience response the main question for the pilot.

What to evaluate

Start with views and profile visits, then look at saves, follows and enquiries that express a specific research need. These are indicators proposed for a future pilot.

Turn a monthly finding into the next editorial brief

Suppose more readers open a long-form article, while fewer reach the end. What should change in the next brief?

Match the format, length and observation window. Keep the topic and revise the lead and subheads. That gives the next comparison a specific editorial change to examine.

Record the change in the calendar. Review opening and completion together when the next comparable piece has had the same time to circulate. One post can guide the next question.

LANDMARK · Mini program acquisition

Research / test planning

Cheaper clicks.
But cheaper acquisition?

The initial problem

The client needed to compare WeChat, Rednote, Douyin and community promotion. Impressions, clicks, registration, coupon redemption and purchases describe different outcomes. CPC or another brand’s ROI cannot settle the budget decision.

My approach

I separated reach, click, mini program arrival and business action. I examined journey friction, user intent, available evidence and total cost independently.

Check what each piece of evidence actually supports

Metric definitions

Search-index growth, relative ROI improvement and absolute sales return are not interchangeable. Revenue divided by ad spend is not profit.

Comparable contexts

Other brands, categories and audiences provide references. The destination journey and conversion objective also need to match.

Complete costs

Account for content, operations, third-party handoffs and data setup alongside media spend. Compare total CPA for the same qualifying business action.

Turn recommendations into a validation plan

  1. Choose one business objective

    Define success for registration, event booking or coupon redemption so channels are judged on the same outcome.

  2. Check the journey and data first

    Confirm destinations, channel parameters, on-device handoffs and conversion reporting before scaling. Obtain Douyin service quotes and validate its route first.

  3. Separate friction from intent

    Measure arrival rate, post-arrival action rate and final cost separately. Smooth arrival and stronger intent are different hypotheses.

  4. Let testing guide investment

    The proposal prioritises WeChat and Rednote to establish a baseline. Other channels depend on verifiable journeys, costs and measurement.

Existing deliverables

What was prepared

A comparison of journeys, reference-case relevance, cost components and test priorities supports a validate-first budget recommendation.

Validation boundary

What remains to be tested

No comparable cross-platform campaign results are available in the project material. This case presents research and test design; assumptions, supplier feedback and other brands’ results are not claimed as client outcomes.

What arrival rate would change the recommendation?

Assume the same registration goal and a 10% registration rate after arrival. Route A costs ¥3 per click and 90% arrive. If Route B costs ¥2 per click, what arrival rate does it need to break even?

The threshold for B is 60%. At that point both routes cost about ¥33.3 in media per registration.

The threshold gives the pilot a measurement brief. Count who actually reaches the mini program after a click, then who completes the same action. Add third-party fees and content costs for the full budget.

BABOLAT · Customer support

Service flow / pilot proposal

Connect finding help
to receiving support.

The existing case focuses on the handoff from the mini program to WeCom support. I extended the flow into intake, routing and follow-up so reviewers could discuss both the user’s request and the team’s responsibility.

User action

Look for help in the mini program

Enter support and describe the need

Wait for a reply and resolution

Team response

Clarify the entry point and reception

Discuss routing and assigned staff

Define ongoing follow-up ownership

Review checkpoint

Is help easy to find?

Does someone receive the request?

Does ownership survive the handoff?

Walk through the moment responsibility changes hands

Pilot scenario. A user enters support from the mini program, describes a problem and needs a second team member to follow up.

Break the transfer into two actions. The first responder records what is already known. The receiving colleague confirms who will reply. From the user’s side these steps should feel like one conversation.

Pause the walkthrough at the transfer. Can the receiving colleague see what the user has already explained? If nobody picks it up, can the team see that the request is still open? These are practical questions for the pilot.
